### Australia Unveils Plan Allowing Users to Opt Out of Social Media Algorithms

We mentioned the federal government's digital duty of care laws on Monday, and we got a bit more information on this yesterday, when Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and the Communications Minister Anika Wells **unveiled** their plan to give Aussies over the age of 16 years old the ability to decide the type of content that they see in their social media feeds. But what this means is that social media companies like Facebook, Instagram, X, and TikTok will be required to send Aussie users a push notification, giving them the choice to opt out of their algorithms and **revert** back to the old **chronological** system, where you only saw content from accounts that you'd actively decided to follow. So the goal here is reducing exposure to harmful content online. And that's because some research and some advocates have pointed out that algorithms, which show you things that you didn't necessarily decide actively to see, are responsible for pushing **misogynistic** and extreme content at young men, in particular in some cases.

And the goal is to try and protect people under 18—so quite a small age sample there, 16-year-olds to 18-year-olds—from some of this harmful content. Now, that includes material promoting eating disorders, hostile ideas about women and gender equality, and anything **glorifying** crime or life-**endangering** **stunts**.

Right. And the details are still being worked out, but PM Albanese says the **onus** will be on Big Tech companies to comply with the new rules. And if they don't, they could cop **fines** of more than $100 million.

As for how it landed, consent advocate Chanel Contos, who called for an opt-in version of this law last week, said it was a great first step, but it's not been welcomed by everyone. No, it hasn't. So the Coalition **reckons** it's a threat to free speech.

It says that it gives too much discretion to Minister Wells to decide what is and isn't harmful. The Prime Minister has rejected that. He says it's not about giving government control, it's about giving people control—those were his words.

So in terms of what happens now, the bill is out for consultation before it gets introduced to Parliament later in the year.
