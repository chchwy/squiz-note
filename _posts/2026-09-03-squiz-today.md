### Independent Review Blames Outdated GPS Card and Staff Shortages for Telstra Network Outage

The findings from an independent review into Telstra's network **outage** on the 8th of July have been released. So the key findings from the report are that Telstra didn't have enough engineering staff and didn't treat its timing system, which triggered the whole network failure, as a priority.

Which is obviously an ongoing issue in Australia at the moment due to the Optus 000 **outage** that happened the year prior. So as well as that, businesses were unable to process payments, public transport was affected as well. So there was lots going on and it was all due to this timing system at the centre of things.

So the report put the **outage** down to a software upgrade—those **pesky** software **upgrades**—that wasn't done on a Global Positioning System, or GPS, card, which is a very important piece of hardware because it essentially keeps the exact time using information from **satellites**. Yeah, it's what the phone networks run off to stop the signals from **overlapping**. Now, if learning all about how phone systems operate wasn't on your **bingo** card for this year, it wasn't on ours either, but here we are.

So because that little piece of hardware **hadn**'t been updated, the card hit a limit and it reset back 20 years. So when **timestamped** calls started coming through as 2026, the system saw them as errors, and, in a **domino** effect, started blocking them, and around 45% of the network went down. So now, coupled with that was the fact that the two senior engineers in the whole company who knew how to fix the problem, they'd worked overnight and they were sent home to get mandatory rest.

So they were asleep when the system crashed. And the **upshot** of that is that the report found that Telstra had too few engineering staff and **insufficient** expertise—that's how it framed it—to support the timing system. Yeah, so Telstra's boss Vicky Brady was talking about this yesterday.

She said that the company had taken stock of the findings and says more expert staff have been added to the team that manages the timing system, so they're not just dependent on those two engineers, which is a problem if they're on leave or asleep. So it's also introduced extra monitoring and **alarms** so this sort of thing **doesn**'t happen again.
