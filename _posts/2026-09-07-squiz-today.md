### United Nations Adopts New World Map Projection Correcting Continental Sizes

Weirdly enough, we've been talking about maps a whole bunch over the last couple of weeks, what with the Lake Ontario, Lake America thing that's been going on between the US and Canada, but the UN has got on board with a change to a major map. It's actually changing kind of the go-to world map that you might have grown up looking at. It's come up with a new version that gives us a better idea of how big the **continents** on Earth actually are.

Andrew, I didn't think we'd be talking this much about maps on The Squiz, but here we are; this is where we've arrived. So the idea behind this is to make the world map more accurate, and Africa has been the driving force behind the proposal, which the UN accepted on Friday, because it had actually been **massively** **downsized** in the map we know. Now, I looked up the official term for people who love looking at maps, and it's a "cartophile", whereas someone who actually makes or draws maps is a "**cartographer**".

So, fun fact: I'm a cartophile who struggles to actually follow maps. I can't follow a map to save my life. The original map that you probably know of is called the Mercator **projection**.

It's named after a **cartographer** who came up with it a long time ago, but it does kind of **distort** the size of various **continents**, which is why this change has been made. So Africa looks much, much bigger in the new version of the map. A little bonus for us, Australia looks bigger as well.

Now, there's a reason that this has been done. It's not just for fun. Advocates for this change say that **diminishing** Africa's size also **subliminally** **diminishes** its importance on the world stage.

And beyond that, the map is just more accurate than it previously was. Now, worth noting, the only country to vote no to this resolution was the US, and **coincidentally** or not, the US looks significantly smaller on this new version of the map. So we'll put a link in the episode notes in case you'd like to compare and contrast.
